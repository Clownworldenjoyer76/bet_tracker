Phase 1 — Data Pipeline
Parse & consolidate all 450+ files into a single structured dataset (Parquet or SQLite). Normalize fighter names (casing, nicknames). Output: one row per fight with all fields.
Feature engineering per fighter — computed from historical records prior to each fight date (no leakage):

Win rate (last 5, last 10, all-time)
Finish rate (KO/TKO vs decision)
Opponent quality (avg win% of who they beat/lost to)
Days since last fight
Win/loss streak
Sportsbook implied probability (derived from moneyline)


Phase 2 — Model
Target variable: result_fighter_1 (binary win/loss)
Baseline first: Logistic regression on implied probability alone. This is your benchmark — it's hard to beat consistently.
Then try: XGBoost or LightGBM with all engineered features. These handle tabular sports data well.
Key concern — calibration: You want calibrated probabilities, not just accuracy. Use Brier score and log loss as metrics, not just accuracy.
Validation: Time-based split only. Never random split — future fights can't train past predictions. Train on years 1–5, test on year 6.

Phase 3 — Edge Detection (the actual goal)
Raw prediction accuracy isn't enough. You need edge over the sportsbook:
edge = model_probability - implied_probability
Only bet when edge > threshold (e.g., >5%). Backtest this on your holdout year.
Track:

ROI by edge bucket
ROI by favorite vs underdog
ROI by weight class (some are more predictable than others)


Phase 4 — Betting Simulation
Use Kelly Criterion (fractional Kelly, e.g., 25%) for sizing. Never flat bet without validating edge first.
Backtest on year 6 with realistic assumptions: -110 juice on even lines, closing line odds (not opening).

What you're missing that would help

Fighter attributes: age, reach, height, stance, record at fight time
Fight context: title fight, main card vs prelim, weight class
Closing line vs opening line movement (sharp money signal)


Honest caveat
UFC is harder to model than team sports. Small sample per fighter, high variance outcomes, and sportsbooks are sharp. Consistent edge of even 3–4% ROI would be genuinely good. The model's main value is identifying mispriced underdogs, not picking favorites.
